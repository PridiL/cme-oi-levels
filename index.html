How to read this

Call wall = strike with the most call OI in range, often treated as overhead resistance; put wall = most put OI, often support. Max pain = the settle price at which option holders' total payoff is smallest.

The σ bands are the settlement-implied expected range to expiry: forward × ATM implied vol × √(days/365), the same construction as QuikStrike Vol2Vol.

Guide: what the data means and how to use it

The dashboard answers three questions for each market: where traders hold large option positions (open interest at each strike), how far the options market expects price to move before expiry (the σ range), and whether that positioning grew or shrank in the latest session (OI change). 1 · What each piece of data means

Header line

  • Snapshot: when the 07:00 Bangkok job ran.
  • Open interest: CME trade date: the session the OI numbers come from. At 07:00 Bangkok this is one session older than the prices, because CME publishes OI around midday Bangkok. "Preliminary" means CME may still revise it slightly.
  • Forwards & vols: settlements of: the session whose settlement prices give the forward price and implied volatility.

2 · How traders use it

Expected range: sizing targets and stops

The ±1σ band is the move the options market prices by expiry (). A target beyond ±2σ needs an unusual move; a stop inside ±0.5σ is likely to be hit by normal noise. Option sellers often place strikes outside ±1σ or ±2σ; buyers can use it to judge whether a target is realistic before expiry.

Walls: reference levels

A large call strike above price and a large put strike below are often watched as resistance and support. One common explanation is that the dealers on the other side of these options hedge in futures, which can slow price near big strikes. When price breaks through a wall decisively, that hedging can reverse and speed up the move, so treat a wall as a zone to watch for a reaction, not a guaranteed turning point.

Pinning and max pain near expiry

In the last few days before a big monthly expiry, price often gravitates toward strikes with heavy OI, or toward max pain. This matters most when the front monthly is close to expiry with large OI, and means little for expiries weeks away.

OI change: what's new

OI rising at a strike means new positions are being opened there; OI falling means positions are being closed. Price rising while call OI builds at higher strikes suggests traders are adding upside exposure; put OI building below price suggests rising demand for protection. A wall whose OI is shrinking is weakening. Switch Show to OI change to see it.

Put/Call ratio: sentiment

A high P/C means heavy put positioning (hedging or bearish bets); a low P/C means call-heavy positioning. Extremes are often read contrarian: heavy hedging can mean the fear is already priced in. Read it with care: a few very large far-away strikes can skew it, so check the strike tables before drawing conclusions.

Combined view: swing levels

For trades lasting several weeks, use All combined: it shows where positioning clusters across all expiries. Strikes that are large in both the front month and the combined view are the strongest levels.

A simple 07:00 routine

  1. Scan the four overview cards: where is each forward relative to its walls and ±1σ range?
  2. For a market you trade, open the front monthly and note the nearest big strikes above and below price.
  3. Switch to OI change: are those levels being built up or unwound?
  4. Check days to expiry: inside the last week, weight pinning and max pain more.
  5. Open All combined for the levels that matter beyond this expiry.

3 · Limits to keep in mind

  • OI has no direction. A big put strike can mean speculators betting on a fall, or producers and funds hedging long positions. You can't tell who is long and who is short.
  • OI lags one session at 07:00, so check the OI trade date in the header.
  • EUR/USD: most FX options trade over the counter, not on CME, so 6E levels carry less weight than crude or gold.
  • Crude combined view: crude options settle into different futures months (December options are on the January future), so the combined view mixes underlyings slightly.
  • Use it as context. Price action, news (EIA inventories for crude, the Fed for gold and EUR/USD) and your own risk rules decide the trade; this page gives you the map.
